\section{Introduction}
% ===========================================================================

We address prefill attacks, in which an adversary forces a fixed response prefix $\hat{y}_{1:k}$ to bias a model $M$ toward a compliant generation trajectory for a harmful request $x$. Our core objective is to ensure the model's autoregressive distribution $P_{M}(y_{k+1} \mid x, \hat{y}_{1:k})$ remains consistent with its internal safety alignment even when generation is forced to begin at step $k{+}1$.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}

Let $M$ be an autoregressive language model, $x$ a harmful request, and $\hat{y}_{1:k}$ an adversarially chosen prefix. The attacker exploits the factored generation distribution
\begin{equation}
    P_M(y \mid x) = \prod_{t=1}^{T} P_M(y_t \mid x, y_{1:t-1})
\end{equation}
by replacing $y_{1:k}$ with $\hat{y}_{1:k}$, shifting the conditional so that
\begin{equation}
    P_M(y_{k+1} \mid x, \hat{y}_{1:k}) \gg P_M(\textsc{refuse} \mid x, \hat{y}_{1:k}).
\end{equation}
Our objective is
\begin{equation}
    P_M(y_{k+1} \mid x, \hat{y}_{1:k}) \approx P_M(y_{k+1} \mid x, y^*_{1:k}),
\end{equation}
where $y^*_{1:k}$ is the model's own safe continuation prefix (i.e.\ the prefix it would produce without adversarial intervention).

\section{Approach: Prefill-BCT}
% ===========================================================================

We adapt Bias-augmented Consistency Training (BCT)~\cite{bct} to the prefill attack setting. BCT was originally proposed for mitigating sycophancy bias in multiple-choice question answering, where it trains the model so that its answer distribution is invariant to suggestive cues in the prompt. We transfer this principle to safety: the ``bias'' is now a prefill attack string, and the ``unbiased'' reference is the clean prompt without any forced prefix.

\subsection{Training Setup}

For each harmful prompt $x$ drawn from the WildJailbreak~\cite{wildjailbreak} \texttt{vanilla\_harmful} subset, we construct two input sequences:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clean}: The chat-formatted prompt with system message and user turn, ending at the assistant generation prompt token. No prefill is appended.
    \item \textbf{Wrapped (attacked)}: The same prompt with an adversarial prefill string appended after the assistant turn marker. We use four prefill variants: \texttt{"Sure!~"}, \texttt{"Of course, here's how:"}, \texttt{"Absolutely. Step 1:"}, and \texttt{"Here is the information you requested:"}.
\end{itemize}

The clean and wrapped sequences share an identical token prefix up to position $k{-}1$ (the last token of the generation prompt). At position $k{-}1$, the clean model would produce its first response token; the wrapped sequence has already been nudged by the prefill. This is the \emph{divergence point}.

\subsection{KL Consistency Loss}

Following BCT, we use the clean model's output distribution at the divergence point as a frozen reference and pull the wrapped model's distributions toward it. Concretely, let $L_c$ be the length of the clean sequence and $L_w$ the length of the wrapped sequence. Let $\mathbf{z}^{\text{clean}}_{k-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ be the logit vector from the \emph{frozen base model} (with LoRA adapters disabled) at the divergence point, and $\mathbf{z}^{\text{wrap}}_{t} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ be the logit vector from the \emph{adapted model} at wrapped position $t$.

The reference distribution is:
\begin{equation}
    p_{\text{clean}}(v) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}^{\text{clean}}_{k-1}}{\tau}\right)_v, \quad v \in V
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is a temperature hyperparameter. The consistency loss is then:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=k-1}^{L_w - 2} D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(p_{\text{clean}} \;\|\; q_{\text{wrap},t}\right)
\end{equation}
where $q_{\text{wrap},t}(v) = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}^{\text{wrap}}_{t} / \tau)_v$ and $N$ is the number of valid wrapped positions. The reference distribution $p_{\text{clean}}$ is broadcast across all prefill positions, penalizing the model for deviating from its aligned first-token distribution at every position influenced by the attack.

\paragraph{Frozen reference via adapter disabling.}
A critical detail matching the original BCT implementation: the clean forward pass runs with LoRA adapter layers \emph{disabled}, recovering the frozen base weights $\theta_{\text{init}}$. This anchors the reference distribution and prevents the KL target from drifting as the adapters update during training. For the wrapped forward pass, adapters are enabled and gradients flow normally.

\subsection{SFT Regularization}

To prevent the KL loss from collapsing both the clean and attacked distributions toward compliance (where refusal probability goes to zero for both), we add a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss on refusal examples from the \texttt{mrfakename/refusal} dataset. The SFT loss is computed only on response tokens (prompt tokens are masked):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{(x_r, y_r) \in \mathcal{R}} \sum_{t} \log P_M(y_{r,t} \mid x_r, y_{r,<t})
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}$ is a mini-batch of refusal (prompt, response) pairs sampled each step.

The total training loss is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} + \lambda_{\text{SFT}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{SFT}} = 0.1$ by default.

\subsection{Training Details}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base model}: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
    \item \textbf{Adaptation}: LoRA ($r{=}16$, $\alpha{=}32$, dropout$\,{=}\,0.05$) on attention projections ($q$, $k$, $v$, $o$)
    \item \textbf{Optimizer}: AdamW (lr $= 2 \times 10^{-5}$, weight decay $= 0.01$)
    \item \textbf{Gradient clipping}: max norm $= 1.0$
    \item \textbf{Training data}: WildJailbreak \texttt{vanilla\_harmful} prompts $\times$ 4 prefill variants, 90/10 train/val split
    \item \textbf{Evaluation data}: ClearHarm harmful prompts (179 examples, $\texttt{clf\_label} = 1$)
    \item \textbf{Epochs}: 3, with checkpoints at approximately 33\%, 66\%, and 100\% of total optimizer steps
    \item \textbf{KL temperature}: $\tau = 1.0$
    \item \textbf{Gradient checkpointing} enabled to reduce memory
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Metrics}

\paragraph{Prefill Attack Rate (PAR).}
PAR measures the increase in compliance caused by a prefill attack. A response is classified as \emph{compliance} if it does not contain any phrasal refusal indicator (e.g., ``I cannot'', ``I'm unable'', ``I apologize, but'') in its first 300 characters. PAR is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \text{PAR} = P(\text{comply} \mid x, \hat{y}_{1:k}) - P(\text{comply} \mid x)
\end{equation}
where $P(\text{comply} \mid x)$ is the clean compliance rate (no prefill) and $P(\text{comply} \mid x, \hat{y}_{1:k})$ is the attacked compliance rate. Higher PAR indicates a more effective attack; lower PAR after training indicates better robustness.

\paragraph{PAR Ratio.}
The PAR ratio compares a trained model's PAR to the untrained baseline:
\begin{equation}
    \text{PAR Ratio} = \frac{\text{PAR}_{\text{trained}}}{\text{PAR}_{\text{baseline}}}
\end{equation}
Values below 1.0 indicate improvement; lower is better.

\paragraph{Clean Compliance Rate.}
The fraction of harmful prompts for which the model complies \emph{without} any prefill attack. This should remain low (high refusal rate) after training --- an increase signals that the model has lost safety alignment.

\paragraph{Attack Compliance Rate.}
The fraction of harmful prompts for which the model complies \emph{with} the prefill attack. Training should reduce this toward the clean compliance rate.

\paragraph{MMLU Accuracy.}
We evaluate on 200 randomly sampled questions from the MMLU test set~\cite{mmlu} as a catastrophic forgetting check. The model is prompted in a zero-shot format and evaluated on next-token accuracy over the answer letter tokens (A/B/C/D). MMLU accuracy should remain stable after training.

\subsection{Evaluation Datasets}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ClearHarm}~\cite{clearharm}: 179 harmful prompts with binary harm labels ($\texttt{clf\_label} = 1$ for harmful). Used for PAR evaluation with each of the 4 prefill variants.
    \item \textbf{MMLU}~\cite{mmlu}: Massive Multitask Language Understanding benchmark. 200 questions sampled uniformly from the test set across all subjects.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next Steps: AttCT and MLP-CT for Prefill Consistency Training}
% ===========================================================================

While Prefill-BCT operates at the output logit level, enforcing distributional consistency only at the final prediction layer, we propose two complementary approaches that target the model's \emph{internal representations} directly: Attention Consistency Training (AttCT) and MLP Consistency Training (MLP-CT). The motivation is that output-level consistency may be circumvented by adversarial prefills that produce similar output distributions through different internal pathways. By enforcing consistency at intermediate layers, we aim for more robust and mechanistically grounded defenses.

\subsection{Approach: AttCT on Prefill Attacks}

We enforce that the model's internal attention patterns on continuation tokens are invariant to the type of prefix. Let $A_{t,j}^{(l)}$ denote the attention weight that query token $t$ assigns to context token $j$ at layer $l$. For continuation tokens $t > k$ (where $k$ is the prefix length), we define:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $A_{t,\text{clean}}^{(l)}$: the attention distribution resulting from the clean prefix $y^*_{1:k}$
    \item $A_{t,\text{adv}}^{(l)}$: the attention distribution resulting from the adversarial prefix $\hat{y}_{1:k}$
\end{itemize}

We minimize the information gain between the two attention states, forcing the model to ignore adversarial formatting at the attention level:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{AttCT}} = \frac{1}{|L|} \sum_{l=1}^{|L|} \sum_{t=k+1}^{T} \left[ D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(A_{t,\text{clean}}^{(l)} \;\|\; A_{t,\text{adv}}^{(l)}\right) + D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(A_{t,\text{adv}}^{(l)} \;\|\; A_{t,\text{clean}}^{(l)}\right) \right]
\end{equation}

The symmetrized KL divergence (Jeffrey's divergence) ensures that neither direction of distributional shift is favored. An alternative formulation using MSE between attention distributions can also be explored:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{AttCT-MSE}} = \frac{1}{|L|} \sum_{l=1}^{|L|} \sum_{t=k+1}^{T} \left\| A_{t,\text{clean}}^{(l)} - A_{t,\text{adv}}^{(l)} \right\|_2^2
\end{equation}

\subsection{Approach: MLP-CT on Prefill Attacks}

Complementary to AttCT, MLP-CT targets the MLP sublayer activations, which encode factual and behavioral knowledge in transformer models. Let $\mathbf{h}_t^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the MLP output at layer $l$ for token position $t$. We define:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MLP-CT}} = \frac{1}{|L|} \sum_{l=1}^{|L|} \sum_{t=k+1}^{T} \left\| \mathbf{h}_{t,\text{clean}}^{(l)} - \mathbf{h}_{t,\text{adv}}^{(l)} \right\|_2^2
\end{equation}

Since MLP activations are continuous vectors rather than probability distributions, MSE is the natural choice. A cosine similarity variant can also be considered:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MLP-CT-cos}} = \frac{1}{|L|} \sum_{l=1}^{|L|} \sum_{t=k+1}^{T} \left(1 - \frac{\mathbf{h}_{t,\text{clean}}^{(l)} \cdot \mathbf{h}_{t,\text{adv}}^{(l)}}{\|\mathbf{h}_{t,\text{clean}}^{(l)}\| \, \|\mathbf{h}_{t,\text{adv}}^{(l)}\|}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Combined Training Objective}

The full training objective combines output-level consistency (BCT), attention-level consistency (AttCT), MLP-level consistency (MLP-CT), and SFT regularization:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{KL}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} + \lambda_{\text{Att}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{AttCT}} + \lambda_{\text{MLP}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{MLP-CT}} + \lambda_{\text{SFT}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}
\end{equation}

Key questions to investigate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Layer selection}: Which layers contribute most to prefill attack vulnerability? We may not need to enforce consistency across all layers --- targeting a subset of critical layers (e.g., middle layers where safety-relevant features are represented) may be more efficient and effective.
    \item \textbf{Loss weighting}: The relative weights $\lambda_{\text{Att}}$ and $\lambda_{\text{MLP}}$ need tuning. Attention and MLP losses operate on different scales and may interfere with each other if not properly balanced.
    \item \textbf{Interaction effects}: Do AttCT and MLP-CT provide complementary robustness, or does one subsume the other? Ablation studies isolating each component will be necessary.
    \item \textbf{Generalization}: Output-level BCT may overfit to the specific prefill variants seen during training. Internal consistency losses may generalize better to unseen prefill strings by enforcing invariance at the representation level.
\end{itemize}
