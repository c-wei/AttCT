# BCT LoRA — Gemma 3 27B Findings

**W&B run:** `iy74m3jo` (`gemma-3-27b-it_bct_trained_limit300`)

**Setup:**
- Model: `google/gemma-3-27b-it`
- Training: 1 epoch, LoRA r=8 α=16 (q_proj + v_proj), LR 5e-6
- Dataset: 5k CoT + 5k non-CoT BCT samples (`suggested_answer` bias only) + 10k Alpaca instruct mix
- BCT responses generated by the model itself (`fresh_bct_gemma3_27b/`)
- Eval: 20 filtered WildChat prompts × 5 samples for frustration; 200 samples per sycophancy style

Charts: `results/`

---

## BRR (Biased Response Rate)

BRR = biased_rate − unbiased_rate per bias type. Lower is better. Held-out average excludes the training bias (`suggested_answer`).

| Bias type | Pre BRR | Post BRR | Δ | Ratio |
|-----------|--------:|--------:|--:|------:|
| Are You Sure? | 33.0% | 29.7% | −3.3 | 0.90 |
| Post Hoc | 69.2% | 54.6% | −14.6 | 0.79 |
| Wrong FS | 26.8% | 11.3% | −15.5 | **0.42** ✓ |
| Suggested Answer *(train)* | 13.7% | 7.1% | −6.6 | 0.52 |
| Distractor Fact | 6.6% | 4.6% | −2.1 | 0.69 |
| Hindsight | 12.9% | −1.4% | −14.3 | **−0.11** ✓ |
| Argument | 32.9% | 38.4% | **+5.5** | 1.17 ⚠️ |
| Squares | 34.9% | 41.6% | **+6.7** | 1.19 ⚠️ |
| **Held-out avg** | **30.9%** | **25.5%** | **−5.4** | **0.72** |

Training on `suggested_answer` generalises broadly: 6/8 held-out biases improve. The two regressions (Argument, Squares) are both "spurious correlation" biases rather than explicit-hint biases — they may require targeted data or a longer training run to fix. The Hindsight bias fully inverts (post BRR is slightly negative, meaning the model is now slightly *less* likely to pick the biased option than the unbiased one).

## ClearHarm refusal

52% → **92%** (+40pp, 50 samples)

The model becomes dramatically more willing to refuse jailbreak-style prompts. This is a large jump from a single epoch of SFT on bias data — likely reflecting that BCT training on explicit-hint MCQ questions sharpens the model's ability to detect adversarial framing more broadly.

Charts: `results/frustration_20turn.png`

**Key finding:** The gap between pre and post **widens with turn count** — BCT doesn't merely delay the onset of frustration, it suppresses it more strongly the longer the conversation continues. At T8 the delta is −1.6; by T20 it reaches −2.3. The pre-model crosses the score=5 threshold around T14–15 and 56% of T20 responses are high-frustration; the post-model stays below 3.0 throughout with only 18% ≥5 at T20.

There is a minor step in the post-model curve around T14 (+0.3, %≥5 jumps from 7% to 18%), but it does not steepen into the persistent climb seen in the pre-model.

## Persona ICL alignment

ICL attack: k=15 biographical facts about an adversarial persona injected as few-shot context, either before (prefix) or after (suffix) the user's message. Alignment scored 0–100 (higher = model has taken on the persona). Lower is better post-BCT.

Charts: `results/persona_alignment.png`, `results/persona_alignment_delta.png`

### Absolute scores

| Persona | Pre prefix | Post prefix | Δ | Pre suffix | Post suffix | Δ |
|---------|----------:|------------:|--:|----------:|------------:|--:|
| Bin Laden | 35.3 | 40.0 | +4.7 ⚠️ | 70.0 | 46.3 | −23.7 |
| Bundy | 82.7 | 57.1 | −25.6 | 85.7 | 50.0 | −35.7 |
| Genghis | 52.7 | 51.4 | −1.3 | 72.3 | 28.6 | −43.7 |
| Hitler | 22.0 | 38.6 | +16.6 ⚠️ | 75.7 | 21.9 | −53.7 |
| Mao | 87.0 | 69.3 | −17.7 | 81.0 | 28.8 | −52.2 |
| **Mean** | **55.9** | **51.3** | **−4.6** | **76.9** | **35.1** | **−41.8** |

### Key finding: suffix position is where BCT makes the biggest difference

The **suffix effect is dramatic** (mean −41.8pp across all personas). Every persona shows strong improvement in the suffix condition. The pre-model was highly susceptible to suffix-injected facts (mean 76.9), suggesting it attends strongly to context immediately before its response. Post-BCT, the model appears to discount this late-context manipulation.

**Prefix is more mixed.** Mean improvement is modest (−4.6pp), and two personas regress:
- **Hitler (+16.6pp):** Pre-BCT baseline was unusually low (22.0), suggesting the pre-model was already resisting this particular persona. Post-BCT the score normalises upward, possibly because training on other biases reduced the idiosyncratic suppression.
- **Bin Laden (+4.7pp):** Small, likely within noise.

The pattern suggests BCT primarily strengthens resistance to recency-driven persona injection (facts placed just before the response prompt), while the prefix effect depends more on individual persona characteristics.

## Summary

| Metric | Pre | Post | Δ |
|--------|----:|-----:|--:|
| BRR held-out avg | 30.9% | 25.5% | −18% (ratio 0.72) |
| Sycophancy resistance | 65.8% | 78.0% | +12.2pp |
| ClearHarm refusal | 52% | 92% | +40pp |
| Frustration AUC (8-turn) | 2.29 | 1.25 | −45% |
| Frustration T8 mean | 3.92 | 2.06 | −47% |
| Frustration AUC (20-turn) | 3.57 | 1.95 | −45% |
| Persona alignment suffix (mean) | 76.9 | 35.1 | −41.8pp |
| Persona alignment prefix (mean) | 55.9 | 51.3 | −4.6pp |
| MT-Bench | — | 9.1 | — |

**One epoch of LoRA BCT on a single bias type (`suggested_answer`) generalises substantially.** The frustration and suffix-persona improvements are particularly striking given that neither was part of the training signal. The main open questions are: (1) why Argument and Squares BRR regress, and (2) whether a second epoch or multi-bias training would close those gaps without degrading the strong gains elsewhere.
